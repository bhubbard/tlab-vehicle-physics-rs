The Secret of Drift Force Feedback

At low slip angles ($\alpha < 5^\circ$), pneumatic trail acts behind the tire patch, producing strong self-centering torque ($M_z$). As slip approaches peak grip ($\approx 7^\circ$), $M_z$ peaks and then rapidly decays to near-zero as trail vanishes. In deep drift angles ($\alpha > 12^\circ$), torque relaxes, naturally rotating the steering wheel into counter-steer!

Hans B. Pacejka Magic Formula

Semi-empirical formulation governing tire adhesion through slip stiffness ($B$), curve shape ($C$), peak friction ($D$), curvature ($E$), and horizontal/vertical shifts.

$$y(x) = D \sin(C \arctan(Bx_1 - E(Bx_1 - \arctan Bx_1))) + S_v$$
⚡

Slip Angle & Ratio Kinematics

Calculates longitudinal slip ratio $\kappa$ and lateral slip angle $\alpha$ with numerical low-speed singularity regularization to guarantee rock-solid standstill stability.

$$\kappa = \frac{\omega r - v_x}{\max(|v_x|, v_{\text{reg}})}, \quad \alpha = \arctan\left(\frac{v_y}{|v_x|}\right)$$
⭕

Combined Slip & Friction Ellipse

Coupled Pacejka interaction attenuation factors $G_{xa}$ and $G_{yk}$. Acceleration wheelspin attenuates lateral cornering grip; lateral slide reduces braking capability.

$$F_x = F_{x0} \cdot G_{xa}(\alpha), \quad F_y = F_{y0} \cdot G_{yk}(\kappa)$$
🔄

Pneumatic Trail & Counter-Steer

Realistic contact patch center-of-pressure offset. Produces accurate self-aligning torque ($M_z$) for force feedback steering wheels and authentic drift counter-steer.

$$M_z = -t(\alpha) \cdot F_y + M_{zr}(\alpha, \gamma)$$
⚖️

Normal Load Sensitivity

Tire friction coefficient diminishes as normal load $F_z$ increases, accurately simulating vehicle weight transfer, body roll, and understeer/oversteer balance.

$$\mu(F_z) = \mu_0 \cdot \left(1 - k_{\text{load}} \cdot \frac{F_z - F_{z0}}{F_{z0}}\right)$$
🏎️

Progressive Drift Breakaway

Smooth exponential transition from static peak friction $\mu_{\text{peak}}$ towards kinetic sliding friction $\mu_{\text{slide}}$ when tire traction limit is exceeded.

$$\mu_{\text{eff}} = \mu_{\text{slide}} + (\mu_{\text{peak}} - \mu_{\text{slide}}) \cdot e^{-\beta (\text{util}-1)^2}$$

Integration

Quick Start Guide

Add tlab-vehicle-physics to your project and evaluate full tire kinematics in 5 lines of Rust.

Cargo.toml 
[dependencies]
tlab-vehicle-physics = "0.1"
glam = "0.29"
main.rs 
use tlab_vehicle_physics::prelude::*;

fn main() {
    // 1. Instantiate a high-performance tire model with Pacejka coefficients
    let tire = TireModel::default();

    // 2. Supply instantaneous wheel kinematics from physics engine
    let angular_velocity = 32.0;                // rad/s (~305 RPM)
    let wheel_radius = 0.35;                    // 35 cm rolling radius
    let longitudinal_velocity = 10.0;           // 10 m/s (36 km/h) forward
    let lateral_velocity = 1.2;                 // 1.2 m/s sideways drift
    let normal_load = 4_200.0;                  // 4,200 N normal force
    let camber_angle_rad = -1.5_f32.to_radians(); // -1.5° camber

    // 3. Evaluate comprehensive tire kinetics
    let output = tire.evaluate(
        angular_velocity, wheel_radius,
        longitudinal_velocity, lateral_velocity,
        normal_load, camber_angle_rad,
    );

    println!("Longitudinal Force Fx: {:.1} N", output.longitudinal_force);
    println!("Lateral Force Fy: {:.1} N", output.lateral_force);
    println!("Self-Aligning Torque Mz: {:.2} N·m", output.aligning_torque);
    println!("Grip Utilization: {:.1}%", output.grip_utilization * 100.0);
    println!("Is Sliding/Drifting: {}", output.is_sliding);
}

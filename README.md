## Mathematical Formulations

### 1. Full Pacejka 'Magic Formula' Tire Model (`pacejka`)

The Hans B. Pacejka semi-empirical formulation relates slip to tire friction force:

$$y(x) = D \cdot \sin\left(C \cdot \arctan\left(B \cdot x_1 - E \cdot (B \cdot x_1 - \arctan(B \cdot x_1))\right)\right) + S_v$$

where $x_1 = x + S_h$, with:
- **$B$ (Stiffness Factor)**: Governs the initial cornering/braking stiffness $\left.\frac{dy}{dx}\right|_{0} = B \cdot C \cdot D$.
- **$C$ (Shape Factor)**: Controls asymptotic curve limits (typically $1.3 \le C \le 1.9$).
- **$D$ (Peak Factor)**: Peak friction coefficient or maximum force ($D = \mu \cdot F_z$).
- **$E$ (Curvature Factor)**: Governs the shape around peak grip and post-peak drop-off.
- **$S_h, S_v$ (Horizontal & Vertical Shifts)**: Account for tire conicity, ply-steer, and residual moments.

TLabVehiclePhysics C# direct evaluation:
$$F(x) = D \cdot \sin(C \cdot \arctan(B \cdot x - E \cdot \arctan(B \cdot x - \arctan(B \cdot x))))$$

Also includes **`MultiPacejka`**: Piecewise-linear 2D interpolation across tables of Pacejka curves indexed by dynamic operating parameters (such as normal load $F_z$ or slip angle).

### 2. Slip Kinematics (`slip`)

- **Longitudinal Slip Ratio ($\kappa$)**:
  Relative tangential speed difference between tire tread and wheel contact point:
  $$\kappa = \frac{\omega \cdot r - v_x}{\max(|v_x|, v_{\text{threshold}})}$$
  - $\kappa > 0$: Driving wheelspin (acceleration).
  - $\kappa < 0$: Braking slip ($\kappa = -1.0$ at full wheel lock).
  - Smooth low-speed regularization eliminates numerical singularity at $v_x \to 0$.

- **Lateral Slip Angle ($\alpha$)**:
  Angle between the wheel heading vector and instantaneous contact velocity:
  $$\alpha = \arctan\left(\frac{v_y}{|v_x|}\right)$$
  Smoothly saturates to $\operatorname{sgn}(v_y) \cdot 90^\circ$ ($\pm \frac{\pi}{2}$) when $v_x = 0$.

---

### 3. Combined Slip & Friction Circle (`combined_slip`)

- **Tire Normal Load Sensitivity**:
  As vertical load $F_z$ increases, the tire's effective friction coefficient decreases:
  $$\mu(F_z) = \mu_0 \cdot \left(1 - k_{\text{load}} \cdot \frac{F_z - F_{z0}}{F_{z0}}\right)$$

- **Pacejka Combined Slip Coupling ($G_{xa}, G_{yk}$)**:
  Longitudinal force is attenuated under lateral cornering, and lateral grip is attenuated under wheelspin/braking:
  $$G_{xa}(\alpha) = \cos\left(C_{xa} \cdot \arctan(B_{xa} \cdot \alpha)\right)$$
  $$G_{yk}(\kappa) = \cos\left(C_{yk} \cdot \arctan(B_{yk} \cdot \kappa)\right)$$
  $$F_x = F_{x0} \cdot G_{xa}(\alpha), \quad F_y = F_{y0} \cdot G_{yk}(\kappa)$$

- **Friction Circle / Ellipse Envelope**:
  Horizontal forces cannot exceed available traction:
  $$\sqrt{F_x^2 + F_y^2} \le \mu_{\text{eff}} \cdot F_z$$

- **Progressive Slide & Drift Breakaway**:
  When total demand exceeds peak grip, the friction coefficient smoothly transitions from static peak $\mu_{\text{peak}}$ towards kinetic sliding $\mu_{\text{slide}}$:
  $$\mu_{\text{eff}} = \mu_{\text{slide}} + (\mu_{\text{peak}} - \mu_{\text{slide}}) \cdot \exp\left(-\beta \cdot (\text{utilization} - 1)^2\right)$$
  providing authentic, controllable drift handling.

---

### 4. Camber Thrust & Self-Aligning Torque ($M_z$) (`tire_forces`)

- **Camber Thrust ($F_{y\gamma}$)**:
  Lateral thrust force generated by wheel inclination angle $\gamma$ (radians):
  $$F_{y,\gamma} = C_{\gamma} \cdot \gamma \cdot F_z$$

- **Pneumatic Trail ($t$) & Self-Aligning Torque ($M_z$)**:
  Lateral force acts behind the contact center by pneumatic trail $t(\alpha)$:
  $$t(\alpha) = D_t \cdot \cos\left(C_t \cdot \arctan\left(B_t \cdot \alpha - E_t \cdot (B_t \cdot \alpha - \arctan(B_t \cdot \alpha))\right)\right)$$
  $$M_z = -t(\alpha) \cdot F_y + M_{zr}(\alpha, \gamma)$$
  - **Small slip ($\alpha < 4^\circ$)**: Strong self-centering torque returning wheels to center.
  - **Impending slide ($\alpha \approx 6^\circ - 8^\circ$)**: Peak $M_z$ gives crucial tactile warning of grip loss.
  - **Drifting ($\alpha > 12^\circ$)**: Pneumatic trail decays, producing the classic loose/counter-steering feel during drifts.

## Quickstart

```rust
use tlab_vehicle_physics::prelude::*;

fn main() {
    // 1. Create a default high-performance sports tire model
    let tire = TireModel::default();

    // 2. Wheel kinematics and normal load from vehicle state
    let angular_velocity = 32.0;               // rad/s (~320 RPM)
    let wheel_radius = 0.35;                   // 35 cm rolling radius
    let longitudinal_velocity = 10.0;          // 10 m/s (~36 km/h) forward
    let lateral_velocity = 1.2;                // 1.2 m/s sideways drift
    let normal_load = 4_200.0;                 // 4200 N normal force
    let camber_angle_rad = -1.5_f32.to_radians(); // -1.5° negative camber

    // 3. Evaluate complete tire kinetics
    let output = tire.evaluate(
        angular_velocity,
        wheel_radius,
        longitudinal_velocity,
        lateral_velocity,
        normal_load,
        camber_angle_rad,
    );

    println!("Longitudinal Force (Fx): {:.1} N", output.longitudinal_force);
    println!("Lateral Force (Fy): {:.1} N", output.lateral_force);
    println!("Camber Thrust: {:.1} N", output.camber_thrust);
    println!("Self-Aligning Torque (Mz): {:.2} N·m", output.aligning_torque);
    println!("Pneumatic Trail: {:.1} mm", output.pneumatic_trail * 1000.0);
    println!("Slip Ratio (kappa): {:.2}%", output.slip.slip_ratio * 100.0);
    println!("Slip Angle (alpha): {:.2}°", output.slip.slip_angle_deg);
    println!("Grip Utilization: {:.1}%", output.grip_utilization * 100.0);
    println!("Is Sliding/Drifting: {}", output.is_sliding);
}
```
